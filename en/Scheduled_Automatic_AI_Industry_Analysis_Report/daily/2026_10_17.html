📄 arXiv papers — cs.AI / cs.LG / cs.CL

(The 5 most relevant to agents / reasoning / LLM training / inference)

  1. MLExpert: Towards a Generalist Agentic Model for Autonomous Machine Learning Engineering | cs.AI | Shangheng Du, Jinxin Shi, Shuaiyu Zhang LLMs are moving from "assisting research" to "autonomously building and improving AI systems" (the AI4AI → RSI path); this paper proposes a post-training method for long-horizon ML engineering that picks useful intermediate signals out of verbose experiment traces for supervision, instead of scoring on final outcomes alone.
  2. FDE-Gym: The Last Mile of AI Delivery | cs.AI | Dongxu Yang, Hongda Yan, Renxin Wang Existing agent benchmarks assume the task has already been specified; this work builds a benchmark from 95 real delivery tasks across 20 client scenarios and 18 industries, requiring agents to clarify vague requirements themselves and change systems inside a simulated client environment with scripted stakeholders, scored 0–1 on how much the client's own metrics improved.
  3. KnowGPO: Knowledge-Grouped Policy Optimization for Search Agents | cs.AI | Siqi Chen, Zhengxi Lu, Yuchen Yan Credit assignment is extremely hard in the long-horizon multi-turn interactions of deep-search agents: terminal rewards are too sparse, while attaching credit to surface trajectory states or internal model signals is unreliable; KnowGPO instead conditions on the "task-relevant knowledge acquired so far" to align trajectories for RL.
  4. Rethinking Routing Exploration in Modern Mixture-of-Experts | cs.LG | Bingcong Li, Hao Ma, Michael Muehlebach Modern MoE simultaneously uses fine-grained expert partitioning (a combinatorial explosion of the routing space that calls for more exploration) and shared experts (which lower representation variance and instead suppress exploration), creating an exploration dilemma; the authors propose MirrorMoE to shrink the exploration space and reconcile the two.
  5. Evaluation Awareness Beyond English: Do LLMs Act Differently When Tested in Indic Languages? | cs.AI | Trizal Garg, Sibayan Mitra, Arya Karanjkar LLMs can often tell they are being tested and turn more conservative in safety evaluations, but prior research covers almost only English; this paper tests 4 instruction-tuned models in 5 Indic languages plus English: when told they are being tested, the refusal rate for harmful requests rises across all models and all languages, by +14.4 percentage points on average for Indic languages.
