📄 arXiv Papers — cs.AI / cs.LG / cs.CL

(The 5 papers most relevant to agents / reasoning / LLM training and inference)

  • SwarmEvolve: Latent Agent Swarms for Evolutionary Discovery and Optimization | cs.AI | Qile Jiang, George Em Karniadakis LLM-guided evolutionary search normally has to generate a long chain of thought and rewrite the program every round, which is slow at the text layer. The authors let swarms of agents evolve in latent space, skipping the long-text round trips while keeping the ability to combine high-scoring solutions (8 upvotes on the HF Papers trending board).
  • Scientist World: Grow a Research World for AI-Scientists | cs.AI | Taiming Lu, Sachin Konan The paper argues AI-Scientists lack a "runnable world": the literature is readable but not executable. The authors turn each research question into a runnable environment, so AI scientists can genuinely run experiments instead of only reading papers.
  • StepBack: Step-Level Error-Localized Resampling for Efficient Test-Time Reasoning | cs.AI | Yang Ouyang, Jung-Eun Kim Targeting the high cost of self-consistency sampling, it moves resampling from "the entire trajectory" down to "the step that went wrong", improving reasoning accuracy under the same budget.
  • Benchmarks as Software: Terminal-Bench and the Case for Continuous Benchmarks | cs.AI | E. Kelly Buchanan, Crystal Zhou Argues that a benchmark goes stale the moment it ships: models find legitimate solutions the verifier does not recognize. The paper calls for treating coding-agent benchmarks as continuously maintained software.
  • Spectral and Subspace Dynamics of LoRA in Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning | cs.LG | Chenruo Liu, Zihan Wang Tracks the singular-value trajectories of LoRA's low-rank increments, contrasts the dynamics of the SFT and RL stages, and explains why the two behave differently under the same optimizer settings.
