## 📄 arXiv Papers — cs.AI / cs.LG / cs.CL
*(The 5 papers most relevant to agents / reasoning / post-training, submitted 2026-11-10)*

- **[AutoScaffold: AI-Guided Scaffolding for Reinforcement Learning of Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14515)** | cs.LG | Zijian Zhang, Rizhen Hu
  *Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) compresses every attempt into a single scalar and wastes most of the training signal. The authors use AI-generated "scaffolding" to guide the RL process so that information from intermediate steps is used too.*
- **[ReTrace: Distilling Tool-Using Agents into Planner-Free Workflows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14492)** | cs.AI | Kaiyuan Hou, Yang Ping Kuo
  *Enterprises run the same SOP over and over, while a tool-calling LLM agent re-plans every time — expensive and unstable. The paper distills such agents into a fixed workflow that contains no planner.*
- **[Continual-ARC: Measuring Skill Acquisition over a Working Lifetime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505)** | cs.LG | Daniel Lenton
  *Measures the continual learning ability of an agent harness on the scale of "one working lifetime", and points out the limits of existing benchmarks regarding time span and skill accumulation.*
- **[A Theory of Policy Optimization in Post-Training: Coverage Collapse and Reference Resets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14467)** | cs.LG | Audrey Huang, Nan Jiang
  *Revisits the theoretical foundations of policy optimization in post-training, proposing coverage collapse and reference reset as two mechanisms that explain which algorithms can learn near-optimal policies.*
- **[A Margin Perspective on LoRA: Robustness to Catastrophic Forgetting and Adapter Merging (MaLoRA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14397)** | cs.LG | Ziqing Xu, Hancheng Min
  *Explains from a margin perspective why LoRA is naturally resistant to catastrophic forgetting, and improves the merging of multiple adapters on that basis.*
